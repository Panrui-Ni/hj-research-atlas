# HJ Atlas coverage audit (19 November 2026)

This is an **editorial audit**, not a complete census of all HJ research.

## Assessed coverage

- Vanishing discount, contact weak KAM, periodic/stochastic homogenization, coupling, MFG, boundary conditions, singularities: represented by selected works.
- Networks and junctions; nonlinear discount; metric-space Monge solutions; nonautonomous Tonelli evolution; pathwise stochastic HJ; viscous HJ regularity; high-dimensional reachability: newly represented, not exhaustively surveyed.

## Major areas still requiring systematic checking

- Fathi–Siconolfi–Arnaud–Sorrentino weak KAM foundations, invariant Lagrangian graphs, Aubry–Mather geometry, twist maps.
- Imbert–Monneau junction Riemann solvers, flux limits, interface discontinuities and state constraints.
- Lions–Cardaliaguet–Gomes–Cirant MFG master equation, ergodic, singular, displacement monotonicity and homogenization strands.
- Front propagation, level-set equations, anisotropic and nonlocal geometric HJ.
- Second-order/nonconvex stochastic homogenization, random correctors and nonhomogenization counterexamples.
- Numerical methods, verified optimal control, reachability, nonlocal and fractional HJ.
- HJ on metric spaces, graphs, manifolds and infinite-dimensional configuration spaces.

## Minimum acceptance checks for each paper

1. Verify author list, exact title and persistent DOI/arXiv identifier.
2. Compare main theorem with at least one directly preceding result.
3. State the actual mathematical increment without promotional language.
4. Name a nontrivial assumption or limitation.
5. Allow multiple topic tags, but record *logical* edges only after checking.
6. Do not equate this selective bibliography with worldwide publication, author or citation totals.

## Literature search strategy for higher recall

Combine multiple queries by mathematical topic, arXiv identifiers and subject classes (math.AP, math.DS, math.OC, math.PR), author publication pages, review articles and backwards/forwards bibliographic references using zbMATH/OpenAlex/MathSciNet where available. Stop only when successive reference-chain rounds bring negligible new major works; even then make no absolute completeness claim.
